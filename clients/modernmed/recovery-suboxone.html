The basics

What Medication-Assisted Recovery is.

Mainstream, evidence-based medicine — not a moral compromise.

Medication-Assisted Recovery (MAT) combines an FDA-approved medication with clinical support to treat opioid and alcohol use disorder. The most common medication is buprenorphine — the active ingredient in Suboxone (a buprenorphine-and-naloxone combination).

Buprenorphine eases withdrawal and cravings without the highs and crashes of misuse. That stability is what makes it possible to function, work, and rebuild — and decades of evidence support it as a frontline treatment. Where it fits better, naltrexone is also an option; your physician matches the right medication to your situation.

MAT treats a medical condition with medicine and support. It is recovery — not a substitute for it.

The process

How treatment works.

A clear path, with someone alongside you through the part that matters most.

1

Confidential consult

A private conversation about your history and goals. No obligation, no judgment — just an honest look at whether MAT is the right fit.

2

Readiness & COWS check

Your physician confirms timing using the COWS (Clinical Opiate Withdrawal Scale), so your first dose is given only once you're in mild-to-moderate withdrawal — avoiding precipitated withdrawal.

3

Induction

A home or in-office Suboxone induction, with your dose timing and plan set before you begin and a direct line through the first day. Where it fits better, naltrexone is an option.

4

Stabilization

As the dose settles, cravings and withdrawal ease. We fine-tune early instead of leaving you guessing.

5

Ongoing care

Once you're steady, we set a sustainable cadence of medication management that keeps recovery on track.

Questions

MAT & Suboxone, answered.

What is precipitated withdrawal, and how do you time my first dose?

Buprenorphine binds tightly to opioid receptors, so taking it too soon — while other opioids are still active — can briefly worsen withdrawal. To avoid this, your clinician confirms you're already in mild-to-moderate withdrawal, often using the COWS (Clinical Opiate Withdrawal Scale), before your first dose. A short check-in before you start sets the timing for you.

Can I start treatment this week?

Often yes, when clinically appropriate. After a confidential consult, your physician determines the right plan — including whether a home or in-office Suboxone induction fits — and supports you closely through the first days.

Is telehealth available across Illinois?

Yes. Much of MAT care can be delivered virtually for patients across Illinois, with in-person visits at our Park Ridge office when clinically needed.

Do you take insurance for MAT?

The Recovery line works with insurance, Medicare, and self-pay options. We'll walk you through costs transparently at your consult. Exact pricing confirmed at consult.

Is my treatment confidential?

Yes. Intake is private, communication is discreet, and your information is protected under HIPAA. Many of our patients are professionals who need privacy — we're built for it.

Do you treat alcohol use disorder as well as opioids?

Yes. MAT addresses both opioid and alcohol use disorder. The right medication — including buprenorphine or naltrexone — is matched to your situation by your physician.
